## Professor's feedback and direction

### On pure visual similarity being insufficient

A system that only optimizes for visual similarity leads to overspecialization. Example given: for a black, half sleeve Nike shirt, a purely visual system just returns more black shirts or more Nike shirts, technically accurate, but not useful, the customer already has that shirt in mind and gains nothing from near duplicates.

### On popularity bias and the long tail

The system should not just surface highly rated or frequently interacted with products at the top of a category while ignoring tail products. Doing so pushes tail items further into obscurity and doesn't serve the actual goal (increasing sales, helping discovery).

### The actual goal: a hybrid recommender

Rather than only recommending visually similar items, the system should also recommend complementary products, items that go well with the queried product but do not necessarily look like it (e.g. a shirt paired with pants that go with it). The combination of a visual similarity component and a complementary product component, with attention to diversity rather than repetition, is what should be aimed for.
